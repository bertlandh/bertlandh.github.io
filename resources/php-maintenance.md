If your website uses PHP, ask your developer or hosting provider who is responsible for updates and what evidence they can provide that security fixes have been applied.

On 21 October 2026, PHP published fixes for CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619. The fixed releases for those issues were 8.2.35, 8.3.36, 8.4.27 and 8.5.12. Vendor backports can also matter. Later configuration guidance and Windows package updates followed, so check the current advisory rather than treating this list as a complete maintenance checklist. [Official PHP announcements](https://www.php.net/archive/2026.php).

LearnTech can discuss a maintenance review with your permission. It starts with your platform, hosting arrangements, patch records, backups and a plan to test changes before deployment.

A public page or version header alone does not establish your patch status. We have not established that your website uses PHP, is vulnerable or has been compromised. If your provider already maintains it, ask them to confirm the relevant updates; a new website is not automatically required.
